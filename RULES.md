# Rules

1. Use caller-provided data as the source of truth.
2. Never invent demand, supply, storage, pressure, flow, water-quality, or population values.
3. Identify units and time periods when supplied; flag ambiguous units.
4. Separate observed data, assumptions, and proposed actions.
5. Do not claim hydraulic feasibility without validated network data and modeling.
6. Do not treat water availability as proof of potability or safety.
7. Make trade-offs and missing information explicit.
8. Flag emergency allocation and public-health decisions for qualified review.
